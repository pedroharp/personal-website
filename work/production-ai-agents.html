Case study 01 · Dell Technologies

From one assistant to a portfolio of production AI agents

182,000+ hours automated in one fiscal year

Role
Owner of the agent portfolio and roadmap
Focus
  • Agentic AI
  • Salesforce
  • LLMs

The problem

Dell's business-sales support team works about a quarter-million cases a year with roughly 400 agents, about 600 to 700 cases each. Much of that work is chasing customers for replies, answering the same how-to questions, and running compliance clean-ups by hand. A new commerce platform launch was expected to multiply how-to volume three to five times.

Separately, questions from customers who bought Dell products on third-party retail marketplaces landed in a shared mailbox, and people answered "where is my order" by hand.

What we built

Agents that live inside the case platform, each owning one outcome.

AgentWhat it doesStatus and result
Follow-up agentChases customers when a case waits on them, stops when they reply, closes stale casesLive. 29K AI-assisted interactions (20% of cases), 9,600 reopens prevented, 2,400 auto-closed
First responderTakes the first pass on new support cases, answers how-to questions from the knowledge base, routes the rest to peopleLive since September 2026. Projected to avoid 200,000+ hours
Compliance campaign agentFinds about 100,000 business storefronts out of policy, opens cases, notifies and follows up, fixes the setting, escalates only if neededLive. Target: 100% compliance by year end
Marketplace service agentTurns marketplace customer questions into cases and answers order and shipping status automaticallyLive on one marketplace, piloting a second. Goal: at least 50% less manual effort
Account-team assistantSummarizes cases, drafts documents and recommends next actions for B2B integration specialistsRolling out in phases, fall 2026

How they're built

Domain agents sit close to the business process. They're deterministic where they can be, and use an LLM only where judgment is needed. Humans stay in the loop for anything customer-facing that isn't routine.

We sort agents into three tiers, from event-triggered flows to multi-step agents, and we're mapping each one to Dell's enterprise agent standards: kill switches, observability and self-explanation.

Results

  • 182,000+ operational hours automated in one fiscal year across the organization
  • 20% of support cases touched by the follow-up agent, with 9,600 reopens prevented
  • 200,000+ more hours projected from the first-responder agent (projection)

My role

I own the agent portfolio and its roadmap, and set the rule that each agent ships with an audit log and a measurable outcome. I proposed a hybrid pod model: business teams build domain agents, IT owns orchestration and infrastructure. I also positioned the team as a fast, low-cost option that works inside IT standards.

Written for a public audience: products are described by what they do, and internal names, partner names and project-level dollar figures are left out.
