Case study 04 · Dell Technologies

Rebuilding internal work intake with micro-frontends

80% faster submissions, 2,500 monthly users

Role
Architecture and program lead
Focus
  • Micro-frontends
  • Salesforce
  • UX

The problem

In 2018 I stood up a Salesforce Service Cloud instance that now handles about 1 million cases a year for Dell's online business. Teams across marketing, SEO, legal, retail operations and sales use it to request work from about 1,400 agents.

The agents' Salesforce screens worked for agents. The requesters, who only wanted to ask for something, had to fill out long forms built for the back office, with inconsistent fields and naming.

The decision

Nobody at Dell had settled the long-term platform for case management. Rebuilding the requester side inside Salesforce would have tied the front end to a back end that might change.

So we built the requester experience as micro-frontends: small, standalone form apps that create cases through APIs and can be embedded anywhere. One of them now sits inside the sales team's support portal, so reps open a ticket in two clicks without leaving the tool they already use.

What we built

27 intake forms rebuilt as micro-frontends, a shared header built with Dell IT's sales modernization team, and a design pass across 29 forms that cleaned up conflicting taxonomy ("Americas" vs "Latam", for example). A later prototype added an LLM chat layer so requesters could ask about their cases in plain language.

Results

  • Form submission time: about 2 minutes to 25 seconds (about 80% faster)
  • Queue backlog in the same quarter: 372 to 43 cases (88% lower)
  • About 2,500 monthly active requesters on the new forms
  • Design prototype turnaround: 2–3 days to 1–2 days

My role

I chose the architecture, secured the partnership with Dell IT for the shared header, and ran the program with a two-engineer front-end team in Brazil, a PM and a designer.

Written for a public audience: products are described by what they do, and internal names, partner names and project-level dollar figures are left out.
