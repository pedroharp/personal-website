Case study 03 · Dell Technologies

One product, the right content for every channel

12,500+ items across 19 channels

My team built the content layer. Checkout and platform work belonged to other teams.

Role
Application owner
Focus
  • GenAI
  • Content
  • Agents

The problem

Dell sells through its own site, third-party marketplaces, retailers, social and email. Each channel has its own rules: title length, bullet count, banned words, required attributes. Teams wrote one short title to fit the strictest channel and reused it everywhere, so channels that allow longer titles lost keywords. Writing custom content by hand for thousands of products didn't scale.

What we built

An in-house generative AI engine that turns product specs into content for each channel: titles, bullets, descriptions, FAQs and Q&A. It works from:

  • a brand-voice and persona library
  • each channel's rules and character limits
  • SEO keywords
  • product data pulled from the product information system through APIs and event queues

People review the output before it ships, and it passed Dell's review for official company content. We added translation for non-English marketplaces. Later the engine started producing structured product attributes for the feeds that marketplace and retail channels read.

Agents on top

A feed-health agent checks every product feed for coverage, conformance to channel and internal rules, and consistency. Its first run found 1,200 products with duplicate titles, products over a channel's attribute limit, and missing variant IDs.

Results

  • 12,500+ content items across 19 channels for 4,000 products
  • First non-English marketplace launched at production scale
  • When a major marketplace changed its title rules mid-year, the engine rewrote affected titles instead of a manual clean-up

My role

Application owner. I ran an open build-vs-buy test against the product-data vendor's native AI instead of arguing it in meetings. I also set the architecture rule: outside analytics tools can recommend, but the action layer that writes content stays in-house. That protects Dell's content and gets to market faster on an already-approved tool.

Written for a public audience: products are described by what they do, and internal names, partner names and project-level dollar figures are left out.
